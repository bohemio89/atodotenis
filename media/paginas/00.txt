Lehecka clinches SF berth for Czechia
24 Nov 26
David Kane
SHARE
FACEBOOK
X
EMAIL

The 2026 Davis Cup by Generali Final 8 began in Bologna on Tuesday with a blockbuster quarter-final between Czechia and Canada, two former champion teams facing off for the first time.

Jiri Lehecka clinched a 2-0 victory for Czechia with a 7-6 (4), 6-2 win over Felix Auger-Aliassime, following up Tomas Machac’s 6-2, 3-6, 6-2 victory against Liam Draxl.

“It was a super tough match,” Lehecka said on court. “I was forced to play very well facing some break points and some tough situations. Felix is such a dangerous player in these conditions, so I’m very proud with the way I converted the chances I had in the first set and also how I was able to improve my game in the second. I think it’s a great win, not just for me but for the whole team and I’m very, very proud of the team.”

Czechia, who won back-to-back Davis Cup titles in 2012 and 2013, will next face the winner of the Final 8 clash between Italy and Korea, Rep. on Wednesday.

Lehecka came on court an underdog against Auger-Aliassime, who had won their last three matches (including a hard-court victory in Dubai back in February). Still, the 25-year-old kept cool through a tense opening set, serving from behind before ultimately securing it in a tiebreak.

The world No. 14 carried that momentum into the second set, winning the first three games and maintaining the pressure as Auger-Aliasssime served to stay in the match. Down 5-2, Auger-Aliassime saved a match point with a backhand winner, but Lehecka engineered a second as the match hit the two-hour mark. This time, Auger-Aliassime’s backhand sailed wide to put Lehecka and the Czechs over the finish line.

“I think every match here is difficult because the crowd is pushing you,” Machac said in the mixed zone after defeating Draxl. “It's different. It's very different compared to normal tournaments.”

Earlier in the afternoon, the 26-year-old Machac played his first Davis Cup match of the season, his last on-court appearance coming in a heart-breaking deciding doubles match against Spain last year. Though ranked about 100 spots higher than his 24-year-old opponent, Draxl had a force for Canada’s Davis Cup team in 2026, winning key singles matches in their qualifiers against Brazil and France to make it into the Final 8.

Machac got out to a quick lead but Draxl fought back in the second set, leveling the match and keeping things close in the early stages of the third. The turning point came in the fifth game when Machac dug out of a 15-30 deficit on serve. From there, Machac won 13 of the next 14 points to put himself on the brink of victory. Draxl bravely saved a first match point at net but couldn’t save the second, leaving Auger-Aliassime and Lehecka to battle for the second point.

TIE INFORMATION

Venue: BolognaFiere, Bologna, Italy (hard, indoors)

Czechia (No. 5): Jiri Lehecka, Jakub Mensik, Tomas Machac, Patrik Rikl, Adam Pavlasek, Tomas Berdych (captain)
Canada (No. 8): Felix Auger-Aliassime, Liam Draxl, Alexis Galarneau, Duncan Chan, Keegan Rice, Frank Dancevic (captain)

Tomas Machac (CZE) d Liam Draxl (CAN) 6-2 3-6 6-2
Jiri Lehecka (CZE) d Felix Auger-Aliassime (CAN) 7-6 (4) 6-2
Adam Pavlasek/Patrik Rikl (CZE) v Felix Auger-Aliassime/Duncan Chan (CAN) not played
