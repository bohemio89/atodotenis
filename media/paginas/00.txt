Italy solves Czechia, surges into fourth straight final
27 Nov 26
David Kane
SHARE
FACEBOOK
X
EMAIL

Italy is a win away from a fourth straight title at the 2026 Davis Cup by Generali Final 8, clinching a 2-1 win over Czechia after Lorenzo Musetti partnered Andrea Vavassori to defeat Jiri Lehecka and Tomas Machac, 3-6, 6-1, 7-6 (4).

The three-time defending champions have had to battle throughout the week in Bologna, and shook off a second singles defeat from Flavio Cobolli to rebound in doubles, solving an in-form Czech duo in one hour and 56 minutes.

“This has a special meaning in my heart,” a hoarse Musetti said on court with Vavassori. “My last matches in Davis Cup weren’t a win, so I felt like I needed this, to come back and prove that I could help the team. So today, finally, I showed that I’m there and I really care about the team. I’m really happy to be sharing the court with Andrea. We’re such good friends and I’m really happy for everything he’s achieved. So, for me, it was really amazing.

“I don’t have a voice because I screamed all the match, but I’m really happy and proud of that we did.”

Awaiting Italy in the championship tie will be either Great Britain or Spain, who face off in the second semi-final on Saturday.

Musetti was a late addition to the doubles roster, having defeated Jakub Mensik, 6-3, 6-4 to open the first semi-final tie on Friday. Czechia also made substitutions, swapping in Lehecka after his 6-2, 1-6, 6-3 win over Cobolli along with Machac over Adam Pavlasek and Patrik Rikl.

The opening set turned on the eighth game when Lehecka and Machac took a 0-40 lead on Vavassori’s serve at 4-3, ultimately breaking and serving out the set in the next game.

Vavassori, who partnered Matteo Berrettini to win two tiebreaks against Korea, Rep. on Wednesday, helped bring the team back in the second set, scoring a key break of serve in the fourth game and riding that momentum into a winner-take-all decider.

The two teams remained on serve through the first 12 games, with the Czechs saving a break point in the fifth game and the Italians saving two more in the eighth. In a final-set tiebreak, the two teams exchanged a mini-break to deadlock at 3-3 after the first change of ends. A big forehand from Musetti helped the Italians get another lead at 5-4; with the match on their racquet, Vavassori served the team into two match points. One last putaway from the Italians and the Bologna crowd was exultant, eager to see Italy claim a fourth title in a row.

Cobolli was in pole position to secure a 2-0 win earlier in the afternoon, but the 24-year-old lost a second three-setter of the week after one hour and 40 minutes on Friday.

“It’s been a super tough match for me to come back from losing the tie 1-0,” Lehecka said on court. “It’s never easy, but I trusted in me and my game, the way I played this whole week. The way I felt on this court has been quite good. So, I’m super happy with the way it went. The second set was super difficult. Flavio improved his level so much. For me to come back from that second set was super tough, but I tried my best and it was very, very important how I came into the third set to come back, because it’s never easy. But I did well and it’s super good for me that I made this win.”

Lehecka was in a must-win scenario in his first meeting with Cobolli, and looked strong after breaking twice to capture the opening set. Cobolli, who lost a third-set tiebreaker to Soonwoo Kim in Italy’s quarter-final against Korea, Rep., roared back with the loss of just one game in the second, only to fall behind early in the third.

Lehecka saved a break point up 4-2 and, two games later, smacked a high volley into the open court to earn two match points. A final ace put him over the finish line and sent the tie to a final doubles match.

Starting off the tie, Musetti maintained his mastery over Mensik in quicker conditions in Bologna, saving all six break points faced to get the win in 86 minutes.

“I knew that Jakub was a really tough player, especially on this surface, serving bombs from the beginning,” Musetti said on court. “He was averaging three aces every game, so it wasn’t easy to break him. Mentally, I stayed there. I fought, I kind of dealt with all the break points I faced. I’m really, really happy with the energy and with the attitude that I showed.”

A tense start gave way to some stellar Musetti momentum, with the Italian digging out of a 0-30 deficit in the seventh game to win 11 of the next 12 points en route to winning the opening set.

“You have to make the opponent feel that you are always there, even if he’s serving bombs and aces. I tried to change the position, especially on the second serve to not give him targets to have on his serve. I think that was the key of the match.”

The second set played out in similar fashion, with Musetti getting the crucial break at 3-3 and saving two break points in the following game to put himself close to the finish line. Mensik earned another break chance as Musetti served for the match, but found himself out-steadied off the ground as Musetti edged to match point. One last miss from Mensik handed Musetti the win, putting Italy in pole position to reach a fourth straight final.

“I saved a really well-played break point, trying not to rush out. The serve was not really helping me too much in the last game, so I had to fight with the heart.”

Heart proved essential for Musetti over two matches, and will come in handy again on Saturday.

TIE INFORMATION

Venue: BolognaFiere, Bologna, Italy (hard, indoors)

Italy (No. 1): Flavio Cobolli, Luciano Darderi, Lorenzo Musetti, Matteo Berrettini, Andrea Vavassori, Filippo Volandri (captain)

Czechia (No. 5): Jiri Lehecka, Jakub Mensik, Tomas Machac, Patrik Rikl, Adam Pavlasek, Tomas Berdych (captain)

Lorenzo Musetti/Andrea Vavassori (ITA) d Jiri Lehecka/Tomas Machac (CZE) 3-6 6-1 7-6 (4)
