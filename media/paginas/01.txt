Italy survives Korea, Rep. in deciding doubles
25 Nov 26
David Kane
SHARE
FACEBOOK
X
EMAIL

Italy won a thrilling opener to kick off their quest for a third straight title at the Davis Cup by Generali Final 8, surviving an inspired Korea, Rep. 2-1 after winning the deciding doubles match.

With cries of “Italia!” echoing across Bologna, Matteo Berrettini and Andrea Vavassori held firm to defeat Hyeon Chung and Soonwoo Kim, 7-6 (2), 7-6 (11) to book a semi-final meeting with Czechia on Friday.

“It was my dream to play in such an atmosphere at Davis Cup. It was one of my big tournaments growing up, and to play with this guy, we grew up together. We’re almost the same age!” Vavassori said of Berrettini. “It’s really an amazing feeling.”

Vavassori drew from a wealth of doubles experience and electric chemistry with Berrettini to save four set points in an electric second-set tiebreak. Vavassori earned a third match point with a spectacular volley; a point later, they edged the South Korean duo in one hour and 54 minutes.

Kwon attempted double duty after setting the stage for a potentially massive upset when he rallied from a set down to stun Flavio Cobolli, 3-6, 7-6 (3), 7-6 (5) following Lorenzo Musetti’s 6-0, 7-6 (5) win over Seongchan Hong.

Cobolli had been a tiebreak away from clinching a 2-0 victory for Italy but Kwon had won a pair of tiebreaks to level the tie in just under three hours.

Ranked No. 6 in the world, Cobolli was playing his first match since finishing a disappointing 0-3 in his Nitto ATP Finals debut. Across the net was a former world No. 51 in Kwon, who missed the 2025 season to fulfill his country’s mandatory military obligation, though he was permitted to compete in Davis Cup during his ATP hiatus. Kwon reached an ATP Challenger final just before arriving in Bologna, but struggled for consistency early on against Cobolli, who reached his first Grand Slam final at this year’s Roland Garros.

Kwon made a stronger push in the second, and though Cobolli roared back from 3-5 down, Kwon battled back in the tiebreak to level the contest at one set apiece.

A dramatic final set ensued, with Kwon holding a match point on Cobolli’s serve at 5-4 and digging out of a 0-30 deficit on his own serve in the following game. Serving to stay in the match for a second time, Cobolli held on to force a final-set tiebreak.

After twice exchanging mini-breaks to open the Sudden Death, an ill-timed double fault from Cobolli handed Kwon two more match points. Cobolli saved one with an overhead, but Kwon struck one last searing forehand to keep Korea, Rep. alive heading into the final match of the day.

Musetti was first over the finish line despite losing a 6-0, 4-2 lead over his No. 787-ranked opponent, ultimately winning in a second-set tiebreak.

“It was a solid win, not an easy first match because it’s been a while since I played for Italy in Davis Cup and of course playing here at home can put on you a little bit of pressure,” Musetti said on court after the match. “I think, at the end, I had to maintain the calm and play a good tiebreak at the end to manage to get the win.”

Musetti struggled with a thigh injury in the middle of the 2026 season, one that forced him to miss both Roland Garros and Wimbledon.
