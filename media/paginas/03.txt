Jodar comeback clinches tie for Spain
26 Nov 26
David Kane
SHARE
FACEBOOK
X
EMAIL

Rafael Jodar clinched the last of the quarter-final ties at the 2026 Davis Cup by Generali Final 8, rallying to defeat Jurij Rodionov, 2-6, 6-0, 6-1 to defeat Austria, 2-0, and book six-time champion Spain into the semi-finals against Great Britain.

Playing the second match of the day, 20-year-old Jodar suffered a slow start in the second Davis Cup match of his career, but won 14 of 17 points to start the second set and lost just one more game to turn the match around and ultimately claim victory after 91 minutes in Bologna.

“It was a very tough game but having the lead already from the first win in the series, I think really helped me a lot to get used to the court better and play with more confidence, so I’m super happy to be in this position,” Jodar said on court after the match.

Rodionov had been the key player for Team Austria through two qualifying victories against Japan and Belgium, and pushed Jodar as the third set got underway. Jodar saved break point in the opening game and scored the crucial break to race ahead 5-1.

Jodar struck three aces in a row when it came time to serve for the match, saving two break points in the process. Rodionov saved one match point it off a backhand miss, but another two strong serves sealed it for the Spaniard.

“I think the advice that the captain David [Ferrer] gave me really helped me to start the second set with more confidence and more relaxed,” Jodar said. “That’s what I had to do. I was in a Davis Cup match, so I had to enjoy myself and try to play free. That’s what I did best.”

Jodar’s win follows Daniel Merida, who played the key points better to ease past Joel Schwaerzler, 6-4, 6-4 earlier on Saturday. Schwaerzler acquitted himself well in his first Davis Cup match, saving three match points from 0-40 down, but Merida steadied when the match was on his racquet and served his way to victory after one hour and 18 minutes.

“The conditions here for me are a bit tough, a little too fast for me!” Merida said on court after the match. “But I was trying to play as much balls in as possible to let him play a bit because he was missing a bit. That was my tactic for today, and I think I did a great job.”

At 22 years old, Merida is playing his first Davis Cup season, playing in place of Carlos Alcaraz, who was nominated to compete in doubles after arriving to Bologna following his first Nitto ATP Finals title.

“Carlos wants to play but he finished three or four days ago with some pain,” Merida explained, “so he was sitting there, cheering for me. I was a bit nervous because I knew that if we do a great job today, he’s going to play on Saturday, so that was my job for today.”

Jodar was also in Turin to cap off a breakthrough season, and the 20-year-old is eager to end his year on the highest possible note.

“It was a lot of pressure for the young guys,” captain David Ferrer said on court. “It was their first time playing the finals at Davis Cup. I’m very proud of them because these were not easy matches. I’m very happy for them because they handled the emotions very good.”

Spain will next face Great Britain, who knocked out an Alexander Zverev-led Germany, 2-1, earlier in the day.

TIE INFORMATION

Venue: BolognaFiere, Bologna, Italy (hard, indoors)

Austria (No. 6): Jurij Rodionov, Lukas Neumayer, Joel Schwaerzler, Neil Oberleitner, Lucas Miedler, Jurgen Melzer (captain)

Spain (No. 2): Carlos Alcaraz, Rafael Jodar, Daniel Merida, Pedro Martinez, Marcel Granollers, David Ferrer (captain)

THURSDAY, NOVEMBER 24 (START @ 17:15 CET)

Daniel Merida (ESP) d Joel Schwaerzler (AUT) 6-4 6-4

Rafael Jodar (ESP) d Jurij Rodionov (AUT) 2-6 6-0 6-1

Lucas Miedler/Neil Oberleitner (AUT) v Carlos Alcaraz/Marcel Granollers (ESP) not played
