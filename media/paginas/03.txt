Great Britain defeat Germany for first Davis Cup semi-final in seven years
25 Nov 26
Kate Clark
SHARE
FACEBOOK
X
EMAIL

Henry Patten and Neal Skupski took a 6-2 7-6 win over Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puez in the deciding doubles to send Great Britain through to the Davis Cup by Generali semi-final.

British pair Henry Patten and Neal Skupski, who have announced they will be playing together on the tour next year, stepped up to the challenge in the deciding doubles to send Great Britain through to their first Davis Cup semi-final since 2019 where they will face either Austria or Spain on Saturday.

Patten, ranked world No.1, and Skupski at No.3 were the favourites on paper ahead of the tie, but Krawietz and Puetz have a strong track record in Davis Cup and, as is often said, in this competition rankings go out of the window.

"They're a very experienced team," Patten said of Krawietz and Puetz. "They've been playing together a lot longer than us. I think that's maybe an advantage, maybe they don't know what to expect from me and Neil, but they're a world-class outfit. They've had success for many, many years and a lot of success in the Davis Cup."

The British duo got off to a strong start, breaking the Germans twice in the first set before being pushed to a tiebreak in the second. Amid some dramatic net play and skilled courtcraft, Patten and Skupski let one match point go by before sealing Britain's place in the semi-final on the second, fittingly with a laser-like service return from Skupski, which had been an effective weapon for the pair throughout the match.

"It helps having a guy in your team who can hit a return like that on match point," Patten said of his partner.

"We're going to relax tomorrow, practise a little bit and then get ready for whoever we play on Saturday," Skupski said.

Earlier, in his first Davis Cup match as World No.1, Alexander Zverev took just 70 minutes to beat Arthur Fery and get Germany back on track from 1-0 down, taking a point for his team and ensuring the tie went down to the deciding doubles.

With his 6ft 6in (1.98m) frame and huge wingspan, the German dominated from the baseline and at the net, firing down nine aces compared to Fery's two, and winning 85% of his first serve points against the 54% won by the Brit.

"I'm very happy with the performance in general," Zverev said. "The first match here is never easy, but I'm definitely happy with it right now.

"We needed the win and it's going to be a team effort winning here. I'm happy that I could deliver today but we're not through yet. We've got a very tough and deciding doubles to play and hopefully we can manage to win that as well."

Arthur Fery
Photo: Getty Images for World Tennis

In the first match of the day, Cameron Norrie took control from the off against Jan-Lennard Struff and wasted no time in securing an important first point for Great Britain.

From his dominant start in the first set, which he wrapped up in just 23 minutes, Norrie had to up his game in the second as Struff pushed him into the corners with his trademark powerful groundstrokes.

Norrie's response was to break Struff to love for 4-2 to take control once again. From there, victory was in sight and the lefthander closed out the match at the first time of asking.

"I know Struffy was playing well," Norrie said. "He had a really strong end to the season. It's a quick court, not really suited to me. I knew I had to play outside my comfort zone.

"I stuck to it in that game at 2-all, being love-40 down [in the third set]," he added. "Then I just found my tempo. I found my rhythm. I locked it in. I didn't really remember the end of the match."
